What is retatrutide?

Retatrutide is an investigational medicine being developed by Eli Lilly for obesity and type 2 diabetes. It targets three hormone receptors involved in appetite, blood sugar and energy use.

The supplied source article describes it as a triple agonist and mentions the nicknames “triple-G” and “Godzilla” of weight-loss injections. The manufacturer also develops Mounjaro.

Research status

The source describes retatrutide as undergoing phase 3 trials and not approved or available in the UK. These are statements from the supplied article, not independently verified current regulatory information.

How it works

The source describes three hormone pathways. Their combined effects are the basis of retatrutide’s triple-agonist approach.

GLP-1

Glucagon-like peptide-1

Slows digestion and the passage of food through the stomach, suppresses appetite and stimulates insulin production to help manage blood sugar.

GIP

Glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide

Increases insulin release from the pancreas to regulate blood glucose and lowers food intake.

Glucagon

An additional hormone target

The article describes a role in blood-sugar control and breaking down fat into fatty acids that can be used for energy rather than stored.

The article suggests these combined actions may reduce appetite, slow digestion and increase fat use for energy. Targeting more receptors does not, on its own, establish superiority over another medicine.

Clinical trials and results

The supplied article contains both phase 2 and phase 3 reporting. The figures below remain separated by dose, duration and reported trial phase.

Phase 2: 12 mg weekly

The source reports average loss of 24.2% of starting body weight over 48 weeks. In this group, 26% of participants lost at least 30%.

Phase 2: 4 mg weekly

Average reported weight loss was 17% over 48 weeks. The article also reports that:

  • 92% of participants lost at least 5% of starting weight.
  • 75% lost at least 10%.
  • 60% lost at least 15%.

Phase 3 reporting

Later sections report average weight loss of 28.7% over 68 weeks. The supplied screenshots do not provide enough study detail to evaluate that figure independently.

Different study populations, doses and durations are not a head-to-head comparison. These source-reported averages do not predict an individual outcome.

Comparison with other injections

Mounjaro / tirzepatide

Mounjaro contains tirzepatide, a dual agonist acting on GLP-1 and GIP receptors. Retatrutide also targets glucagon receptors. Both are developed by Eli Lilly.

The source reports average weight loss of 22.5% with tirzepatide at 15 mg weekly over 72 weeks. It describes Mounjaro as available on prescription in the UK for weight management and type 2 diabetes.

Wegovy / semaglutide

Wegovy contains semaglutide, a once-weekly GLP-1 receptor agonist. The article describes slower digestion, reduced appetite and blood-sugar regulation.

The source reports a maximum maintenance dose of 7.2 mg weekly and average weight loss of 21% over 72 weeks. It describes Wegovy as approved for weight loss in the UK, unlike retatrutide, and compares it with the reported retatrutide result of 28.7% over 68 weeks.

These doses and approval statements are reproduced source claims, not independently checked prescribing information. Cross-trial percentages do not establish a direct comparison.

Oral alternatives

Wegovy pill

The source describes a daily oral tablet that works like the injection. It says it must be taken on an empty stomach, usually first thing in the morning, at least 30 minutes before food, drink or other oral medicines.

At the highest 25 mg dose, the article reports average weight loss of 16.6% over 64 weeks, compared with 2.7% with placebo.

Foundayo

The article describes a daily small-molecule GLP-1 tablet that suppresses appetite and promotes fullness. It says absorption is not affected by food or drink, with no fasting or food and drink restrictions.

At 17.2 mg, it reports average weight loss of 12.4% over 72 weeks, compared with 0.9% with placebo.

Trial dosage and injection

The phase 2 doses listed in the article are:

  • Starting doses: 1 mg, 2 mg or 4 mg.
  • Weekly maintenance doses: 1 mg, 4 mg, 8 mg or 12 mg.
  • Maximum weekly trial dose: 12 mg.

The article says doses increased every four weeks, starting low and rising gradually to help the body adjust and minimise side effects.

Injection format

Retatrutide is being studied as a once-weekly injection. The source anticipates self-injection if approved, with gradual increases towards a maintenance dose. It says a final schedule would follow approval, with prescribing subject to eligibility assessment.

Side effects and safety

The source describes the most common effects as mild-to-moderate gastrointestinal symptoms, similar to those of Wegovy and Mounjaro:

  • Feeling sick or vomiting.
  • Diarrhoea.
  • Constipation.

It says symptoms are more likely when treatment starts or the dose increases.

Skin sensation changes

The article reports that 7% of trial participants experienced tingling or sensitivity. It states these changes were not severe and did not cause anyone to stop treatment.

Long-term evidence

The source describes long-term safety data as limited. It says fuller information would follow completed testing and approval, with known possible effects included in a patient information leaflet.

Suggestions reported in the source

  • Eat smaller, more frequent meals.
  • Avoid spicy, greasy or fatty meals.
  • Choose plain foods such as toast and crackers.
  • Take frequent sips of water to help prevent dehydration.
  • Rest until you feel better.
  • Try peppermint or ginger tea for nausea.
